The red thread

Seven blocks, one question: who has a claim, and when?

  • A Market (12–15): financing is NPV = 0; money is made on investments. Imperfections (behavioural, signalling, costs) are where financing matters.
  • B Capital structure (16–18): MM: VL = VU; tax favours debt, distress/agency costs penalise → trade-off; asymmetric info → pecking order. WACC & APV.
  • C Governance (19–20): managers ≠ owners → agency problems; shareholders vs stakeholders.
  • D Options (21–23): replication, binomial, Black–Scholes, real options.

 

  • E Debt (24–26): equity = call on assets, debt = riskless bond − default put. Credit risk, ratings, covenants, convertibles, leasing, SPVs, PE layers.
  • F Risk & planning (27–31): hedge only where frictions add value; natural hedge and treasury first, derivatives for the residual. DuPont, PI, forecasts, EFN, sustainable growth.
  • G Control (32–34): mergers create value only via real synergies; takeovers, LBOs, spin-offs, bankruptcy move control. 8 ideas we know, 10 problems we don't.
Threads: agency (15, 17, 19, 32) · option logic (17, 21–24, 27, 33) · MM → tax → distress → trade-off (16–17) · claim & queue (24–26, 33)
A · The market (ch 12–15)

Ch 12 – Efficient markets

  • Financing = NPV 0: selling a bond/share at market price gives exactly the value given up. Investments can have NPV > 0; inefficient markets allow NPV ≥ 0 (issue overvalued shares).
  • EMH: Fama: prices reflect all information. Jensen: no consistent excess returns.
  • Weak (past prices → technical analysis fails) · Semistrong (public info → fundamental analysis fails) · Strong (even private info → insider trading fails)
  • Implications: random walk (serial corr. ≈ 0: MSFT −0.038, IBM −0.019), cycles self-destruct, good company ≠ good investment, hold the market portfolio, Grossman–Stiglitz paradox, event studies.
  • Not implied: stable prices, nobody makes/loses money, agreement, equal returns (risk earns a premium).
  • Inefficient market: overpriced stock → advantage to issuing; myopic markets may penalise long-term investment (e.g. ESG).
  • Evidence: short-term reversal and momentum; active funds rarely beat the index after fees; fast reaction to bids/earnings; some post-earnings drift (underreaction).
  • Behavioural: prospect theory (loss aversion), overreaction/underreaction, sentiment; limits to arbitrage (short squeezes, Royal Dutch/Shell, LTCM); beware data mining.
Exam tip: know which analysis each form rules out; why EMH ≠ "nobody can make money".

Ch 13 – Financing patterns

  • Retained earnings dominate for US non-financials; net equity issues often negative (buybacks).
  • Equity: cash-flow vs control rights, separation of ownership/control, preferred. Concentration highest in Russia, Argentina, Turkey; lowest in USA, UK, S. Korea.
  • Debt: short/long, fixed/floating, currency, senior/junior, secured/unsecured; no vote (except covenants). Debt-like: payables, leases, pensions.
  • Financial system: payments, borrowing/lending, pooling risk, price information. Primary vs secondary markets; banks, insurers, funds, pensions.
  • Bank-based (Germany, Japan) vs market-based (USA, UK); conglomerates, internal capital markets, family ownership.
  • Fintech: payments, P2P, crowdfunding, AI/ML scoring, blockchain, crypto, ICOs.

Ch 14 – Issuing securities

  • Venture capital: idea → plan → seed → Series A (down rounds possible) → B → exit (IPO/sale). VC active in governance. Post-money = value after investment (Aqua $14m; down round $5.6m).
  • IPO motives (CFO survey): shares for acquisitions 59%, market price 51%, reputation 49%, broaden ownership, founder diversification, lower cost of capital, VC exit.
  • Process: underwriter → registration → prospectus → indicative pricing → book building → final price.
  • Costs: spread ≈ 7% small issues, 1.4% for Rivian; % falls with size.
  • Underpricing: positive first-day return; explained by winner's curse (uninformed must be attracted), agency problems. Also hot issues, poor long-run performance, auctions, SPACs.
  • Seasoned sales: public offers (equity costlier than bonds), rights issues (preemptive), private placements. Price reaction: supply effect, signalling (issue when overvalued → price falls), agency.

Rights issue – recurring calculation

\[P_{ex}=\frac{N_{old}\,P+N_{new}\,K}{N_{old}+N_{new}}\]
\[\text{Right}=P-P_{ex}\ (\text{per old share})=P_{ex}-K\ (\text{per new share})\]
  • Value per old share = value per new share × (new/old). Read which one the question asks!
  • Ivanhoe 3 for 20, P 24.73, K 13.93: P_ex = 23.32; right 9.39 per new share.
  • Lafarge 4 for 17, P €60, K €41: P_ex = €56.38; right €15.38 per new.
  • MacKenzie 11m sh, 5:1, K $39, P $42: raised 11/5·39 = $85.8m; P_ex = (42·11+85.8)/13.2 = $41.50; right $0.50 per old.
A fully subscribed rights issue is value-neutral.

Ch 15 – Payout policy

  • Facts: 27.7% do both dividends and buybacks, 36.7% neither. Timeline: declaration, ex-dividend, record, payment. Buybacks more common in US. 93.8% avoid cutting dividends, 89.6% want smooth stream (sticky); buybacks seen as flexible.
  • MM irrelevance: value set by investments, not payout. Dividend funded by new issue shifts value between old/new holders only.
  • Fallacies: need dividends to live (home-made dividends), price = PV of dividends (also with buybacks), dividends safe vs risky gains. DCF: use total payout.
  • Signalling: dividend ↑ positive, cut negative; buyback may signal undervaluation.
  • Clienteles: transaction costs, convenience, self-control.
  • Taxes: Australian imputation: firm pays 30, holder gets grossed-up 100 + credit 30; net 85/70/55 for 15/30/45% brackets → no double tax.
  • Agency: outcome model (strong protection → higher payouts) vs substitution model (payout substitutes weak protection).
  • Life cycle: growth (invest) → maturing → mature (pay out).

 

Why payout matters only in imperfect markets: taxes, information (signals), agency (free cash flow), transaction costs.
B · Capital structure (ch 16–18)

Ch 16 – MM in perfect markets

  • MM I: \(V_L=V_U\). Proof by arbitrage with home-made leverage. "No cheaper to assemble a chicken from parts."
  • Leverage changes EPS and risk, not value. Macbeth: EBIT 1,500, 50% debt @10%: EPS 2.00 vs 1.50, range 0–3.00 vs 0.50–2.00.
  • MM II:
    \[r_E=r_A+(r_A-r_D)\frac{D}{E}\]
    \[r_A=\frac{D}{V}\,r_D+\frac{E}{V}\,r_E=\text{constant}\]
    If debt gets riskier (rD↑), rE flattens. If debt is riskless:
    \[\beta_E=\beta_A\left(1+\frac{D}{E}\right)\]
  • Break-even EBIT (EPSU = EPSL): Orion EBIT/200 = (EBIT−320)/120 → 800; Alpha 1,000.
  • Arbitrage (Ex 3): VL 1,000 > VU 900: sell 1% L (7), borrow 1% of L's debt (3), buy 1% U (9) → profit 1 now, same future CF.
  • Ex 2c: convertible 4.5% blended into rD = 6%, rA = 11.6% → rE = 17.2%.
Tip: draw rE, rD, WACC vs D/E (WACC flat without tax).

Ch 17 – How much to borrow?

  • Tax shield: annual \(t_C\,r_D\,D\) (0.21·0.08·3,000 = 50). Permanent debt: \(V_L=V_U+t_C\,D\) (Alphabet +$20bn debt → +$4.2bn).
  • Limits: need profits, deduction cap (30% EBIT), debt not permanent, marginal rate < 21%, personal taxes: debt favoured if \((1-t_P)>(1-t_C)(1-t_E)\) (example: 0.687 equity vs 0.630 interest → equity wins).
  • Clientele: individuals borrow dearer than firms.
  • Bankruptcy costs: legal fees, fire sales, lost customers/suppliers/staff.
  • Agency – equity: free cash flow, empire building; debt disciplines.
  • Agency – debt (Circular File: debt 50, assets 30 → debt 27, equity 3): risk shifting (invest 20, 20% chance of 90: firm 30→28, equity 3→10, lenders 27→18); debt overhang (project +15 rejected: holders gain +7 but pay 10).
  • Trade-off: \(V=V_U+PV(\text{shield})-PV(\text{distress and agency})\). Safe, profitable, tangible firms → more debt.
  • Pecking order: internal → debt → equity. Profitable firms have less debt; large/tangible more; high M/B less.
Classic: trade-off says profitable firms borrow more, pecking order says less.

Ch 18 – Financing and valuation

  • After-tax WACC:
    \[WACC=r_D(1-T_C)\frac{D}{V}+r_E\frac{E}{V}\]
    Discount unlevered FCF. Valid if project has firm's business risk and debt ratio.
  • Rumolt: E = 26·58 = 1,508, D = 991, V = 2,499; pre-tax 11.22%, after tax (30%) 10.39% (slide typo 2,449 → 10.26%).
  • Valuing businesses: discount FCF at WACC; \(TV=\dfrac{FCF_{H+1}}{WACC-g}\); subtract debt for equity. Multiples: EV/EBIT, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/E, P/B.
  • FTE:
    \[E=\frac{C-(1-t_c)\,r_D\,D}{r_E}\]
  • Practice: add preferred (+rPP/V), include short-term debt, net NWC. Different risk/debt ratio: compute rA → new rE → new WACC (Sangria).
  • APV = base-case NPV + PV(financing side effects). A: +150k −200k issue cost = −50k reject. B: −20k +60k tax shield = +40k accept. Use for changing debt, interest limits, international projects.
C · Governance (ch 19–20)

Ch 19 – Corporate governance

  • Agency problems: low effort, perks, empire building/entrenchment, too little/much risk (options → risk-taking, gambling for redemption), short-termism.
  • Boards: US/UK majority independent (≈85%); size trade-off (free-riding vs complexity); overboarding, staggered boards. Germany two-tier: Aufsichtsrat (≈20, half employees = codetermination) + Vorstand (≈10). France: one or two tiers.
  • Shareholders: voting, dual-class; limited by expertise, influence, incentive; exit vs voice. Control-block value (Nenova): Sweden 1%, USA 2%, Italy 29%, Korea 48%.
  • Other monitors: auditors, lenders (covenants), acquirers.
  • Pay: US highest; shift to stock/options; managerial excess vs market forces; pay for performance/luck; LTIPs.
  • Regimes: Japan keiretsu; Germany banks. Short-termism vs long-term bank systems. Survey: Japan 97% job security > dividends; USA/UK 89% opposite; FR/DE ≈ 60/40.

Ch 20 – Stakeholder capitalism

  • Stakeholders: employees (DEI), customers, suppliers, communities, environment, government.
  • Pro shareholder: value needs stakeholder investment (Enlightened Shareholder Value); government regulates; owners pursue social goals themselves.
  • Pro stakeholder: government fails (lobbying, slow, leakage); shareholders have no comparative advantage; hard to weigh many stakeholders.
  • 3 principles (Table 20.1): Multiplication (social benefit > private cost) + Comparative advantage (social benefit > social cost) + Materiality (creates profit).
  • Law: Delaware shareholder primacy; UK directors consider others; benefit corporations (Patagonia, Kickstarter); B Corps no legal status.
  • Practice: purpose, CSRD, ESG-linked pay, ESG ratings, "end of ESG?".
D · Options (ch 21–23)

Ch 21 – Understanding options

  • Call = right to buy at strike, put = right to sell; writer has obligation. American: any time; European: expiry only.
  • Payoff (X=80) at 60/70/80/90/100/110: call 0/0/0/10/20/30, put 20/10/0/0/0/0. Profit = payoff − premium.
  • Strategies: protective put (insurance, keeps upside), stock + sold call (caps upside), straddle (big moves).
  • Put-call parity:
    \[C+PV(X)=P+S\]
  • Value drivers (call/put): S +/−, X −/+, σ +/+, time +/+, r +/−, dividends −/+. Bounds max(S−X,0) ≤ C ≤ S.

Ch 22 – Valuing options

  • Replication (Amazon, X 140, rf 2%/6m): stock → 116.67 or 168, call 0 or 28. Δ = 28/51.33 = 0.5455; buy 0.5455 sh, borrow 63.64. Call = 0.5455·140 − 63.64/1.02 = 13.98. Put: Δ −0.4545, lend 76.36 → 11.23 (parity ✓).
  • Risk-neutral: use p so expected return = rf, discount at rf. Same value.
  • Multi-period: backwards like a decision tree (u 1.1376, d 0.879).
  • Black–Scholes:
    \[C=N(d_1)\,S-N(d_2)\,PV(X)\]
    \[d_1=\frac{\ln\!\big(S/PV(X)\big)}{\sigma\sqrt{t}}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{t}}{2},\qquad d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt{t}\]
    Inputs S, X, σ, t, r. Put via parity.
  • Ex 3 (S=X=60, σ 6%/mo, t 3, r 1%/mo): rough C ≈ 3.4, P ≈ 1.7 (check official solution).
  • Call riskier than stock? Yes: \(\beta_{opt}=\Delta\,\dfrac{S}{C}\,\beta_{stock}\). Δ in [0,1], rises with S.

Ch 23 – Real options

  • Value of option = NPV with − NPV without. Types: expand, wait, abandon/shrink, vary output.
  • Expand (Nissan LEAF): ZE0 NPV −46m. Right to invest 900m in ZE1 in 3 yrs = call on asset worth 467m (σ 35%, rf 10%). Without option −209m (467 − 900/1.1³). Option +55m → total +9m, go.
  • R&D (pharma): −18m phase II, 44% pass, option on 130m phase III; underlying 0.8·350/1.096⁵ = 177; option 58.4m. NPV = −18 + 0.44·58.4 = +7.7m, go.
  • Timing (green hydrogen): waiting = call; costs first-year CF and competitor risk. p·0.375 + (1−p)(−0.12) = 0.05 → p = 0.343.
  • Abandon (crusher): PV 4.8m, exit 5.2m = put; call 0.49 → put = 0.49 + 5.2/1.04 − 4.8 = 0.69m; value 5.49m, don't abandon yet.
  • Mothballing: reactivate if price > R, lay up if < M. Flexible production: plant runs only when price > 60/MWh: +35.41 vs −24.67 if always on.
  • Limits: complex, unclear cash flows, competitors hold options too.
E · Debt and structures (ch 24–26)

Ch 24–25 – Debt, credit risk, Oracle

  • Oracle: capex $55.7bn vs OCF $32bn → negative FCF; $43bn new debt, $5bn preferred, ≈$20bn equity to protect BBB− (one notch above junk).
  • Credit risk: 5% bond, par 1,000, 20% risk of paying 500: E[pay] = 0.8·1,050+0.2·500 = 940; value 895; promised yield 17.3%, expected 5%. With 8% → price 870.37, YTM 20.7%. Baa spread peaked ≈5.6pp in 2008; defaults come in waves.
  • Default option: debt = riskless − put; equity = call. Circular File 50/1.10 − 18.6 = 26.9. Put worth more with higher σ, more debt, longer maturity, lower asset value, lower r, higher dividends.
  • Ratings: IG = BBB/Baa+. Interest coverage 20.8 (Aa) → 0.2 (Caa–C). ≈ half of Caa–C default in 10 yrs. Recovery: senior secured loans ≈2/3, junior sub ≈1/5.
  • Altman Z = 1.2x1 + 1.4x2 + 3.3x3 + 0.6x4 + 1.0x5 (WC/TA, RE/TA, EBIT/TA, mkt equity/debt, sales/TA). Low Z = high risk.
  • Security: debentures, mortgage bonds, collateral trust, equipment trust, ABS. Call, put, sinking fund.
  • Covenants: limits on debt/dividends, negative pledge, positive (min WC/equity); breach → renegotiation.
  • Markets: public/private, Yankee, Samurai, Eurobonds, global bonds.
  • Convertibles: bond + call. Live Nation ratio 9.2259, price 1,000/9.2259 = 108.39. Value at maturity = max(bond, ratio·S).
  • Bank loans: revolver, bridge, term, syndicated; SOFR (replaced LIBOR). Short-term: commercial paper, MTNs.

Ch 26 – Leasing, AI financing, PE

  • Operating (short, cancellable, lease vs buy) vs financial (long, non-cancellable, lease vs borrow). Right-of-use asset + liability.
  • Good reasons: convenience, cancellation option, maintenance, tax shield to taxpayer, lessor handles bankruptcy. Dubious: "saves capital".
  • Greymare Bus: $100k, 8×16.2k, t 21%. Yr 0: +100 −21 −16.2 +3.4 = +66.2; yrs 1–7: −12.8. Discount at 10%(1−0.21) = 7.9% → NPV −$660 (lessor +$660). Leasing needs an edge (tax, cost, scale, residual). Young unprofitable firm → leasing attractive.
  • Circular AI financing: 2026 capex ≈$697bn. Nvidia→OpenAI (≤$100bn)→Oracle ($300bn)/Azure ($250bn)→Nvidia chips: same capital counted 3×. BIS 2026 lists as top-3 risk. Echo: Lucent 1999 (vendor financing $8.4bn).
  • SPV: separate entity owning asset + debt; bankruptcy remote. AI trinity: GPU cluster + creditworthy offtaker + private credit; GPUs obsolete faster → collateral/tenor debate.
  • PE structure: Fund (LPs+GP) → TopCo → BidCo (senior secured debt) → OpCo. Priority: senior → mezzanine/2nd lien → PIK/shareholder loans → equity. Private credit: direct, illiquid, floating, unitranche.
  • Waterfall (senior 200, mezz 100, equity 150): sale 280 → 200/80/0; sale 420 → 200/100/120; mezz full at ≥300.
F · Risk and planning (ch 27–31)

Ch 27–28 – Risk management & hedging

  • CRO's 3 questions: major risks? paid for taking them? how to control? Keep paid risks, reduce others.
  • Against hedging (perfect markets): costs, zero-sum (forward = 0 at start), shareholders can diversify.
  • For (frictions): distress costs, taxes (progressive), underinvestment/agency, credit access. Same frictions as MM failure.
  • Currency exposure: transaction, translation (booked in OCI), economic (hardest).
  • Interest risk: floating loans, duration/refinancing mismatch, intercompany loans.
  • Internal first: in-house bank, multilateral netting, cash pooling (physical zero-balance: +150,+40,−80,−30 = net +80; notional: no cash moves).
  • Natural hedges: match debt/revenue currency, costs/revenues, rate profile. Potterton: lease PV $17m offset by $17m debt.
  • Forwards/futures: future = standardised, marked to market; forward = OTC. \(F_t=S_0(1+r_f-y)^t\) (CAC 7,500 → 7,537.50). Commodity: \(F_t=S_0(1+r_f+\text{storage}-\text{conv. yield})^t\) (WTI conv. yield 25.7%). F<S backwardation, F>S contango.
  • Exporter: USD 5m, forward 9.80 → SEK 49m always. Unhedged 9.00→45m, 11.00→55m. Missing 55m is a forgone gain, not a loss.
  • Swaps: pay SOFR+margin, receive SOFR, pay 6% fixed → net 6%+margin.
  • Options: put protects sellers, call protects buyers; premium, keeps upside. Wheat: future locks, put gives floor; pick put when volume uncertain.
  • Insurance: pooling; premium includes admin, adverse selection, moral hazard.
5-step policy: identify exposures → net internally → size/horizon → instrument → control (no speculation, basis & counterparty risk).

Ch 29–31 – Analysis & planning

  • Book vs market value: ratios say more about running the business. Areas: performance (MVA, EVA, M/B, ROC/ROA/ROE), efficiency (turnovers, margins), leverage (coverage), liquidity (current/quick/cash ratio).
  • DuPont:
    \[ROE=\frac{NI}{Sales}\times\frac{Sales}{Assets}\times\frac{Assets}{Equity}\]
    Oracle 25.4%×0.26×6.16 = 40.2%; SSAB 5.1%×0.91×1.57 = 7.2%; Axfood 2.6%×2.42×4.92 = 31.2%.
  • Limits of ratios: compare over time and peers; accounting choices, seasonality, window dressing.
  • PI \(=\dfrac{NPV}{\text{investment}}\). Budget 100m: A (100, NPV 20, PI 0.20), B (50, 15, 0.30), C (50, 12, 0.24). NPV rank → A only (20); PI rank → B+C (27). One company WACC is wrong → divisional cost of capital.
  • Three-statement forecast: IS, BS, CF as one system; %-of-sales method.
  • Cash budget: start 10, min 5, flows −40, −15, +55, +20 → −30, −45, +10, +30. Need 35 (Q1), 50 (Q2) → peak 50.
  • EFN: sales 200→250: assets +30 (60%), spontaneous liabilities +8 (16%), retention 0.08·250·0.55 = 11 → EFN = 11.
  • Growth: internal = RE/net assets; sustainable ≈ ROE×plowback (15%×60% ≈ 9%; 14% plan needs more leverage/equity). Levers: cut payout, raise margin or turnover.
  • Working capital: operating cycle = inventory + receivables period; cash cycle = − payables period. WCR = inventory + AR − AP.
  • Surplus cash priority: safety, liquidity, maturity, yield last.
  • Financing growth: Capex + WC − retained earnings; pecking order; cost of capital, control, flexibility, market conditions.
G · Mergers and control (ch 32–34)

Ch 32 – Mergers and takeovers

  • Types: horizontal, vertical, conglomerate; waves → consolidation.
  • Value-creating: scale, scope, vertical integration, complementary resources, better control. DIY test: could shareholders do it themselves?
  • Redistributing: customers, suppliers, employees, target holders, bondholders, government (tax inversion).
  • Destroying motives: surplus cash, diversification, EPS boost, empire building, overconfidence.
  • Bootstrap game: acquirer EPS $2, P/E 20 buys target (earns 200k, worth 2m, P/E 10) with 50,000 shares → EPS 400k/150k = $2.67; value still 6m ($40/sh).
  • Merger NPV:
    \[\text{Gain}=PV_{AB}-(PV_A+PV_B)\]
    \[\text{Cost}=\text{cash paid}-PV_B,\qquad NPV=\text{Gain}-\text{Cost}\]
    Ex: 200, 50, synergy 25, price 65 → cost 15, NPV 10.
  • Mechanics: goodwill = price − book ($18m − $10m = $8m); cash = taxable, shares = tax-free exchange.
  • Control market: proxy contest, tender offer, merger, LBO, MBO. Defences: white knight, shark repellent, poison pill.
  • Who gains: target holders usually; acquirers ≈ break even or lose.

Ch 33–34 – Restructuring & what finance knows

  • LBO: MBO, MBI, BIMBO. Value from discipline, incentives, monitoring; redistribution from employees, customers, debtholders, tax authority. Leveraged recap.
  • PE market: LPs repaid first, then 80/20 profit split with GPs. Exits: sale, IPO, SPAC.
  • Divestitures: spin-off (shares to holders), carve-out (sold to public), asset sales. Privatisation vs nationalisation.
  • Bankruptcy: Chapter 7 liquidation, Chapter 11 reorganisation; concerns: gambling for resurrection, absolute priority deviations, tax-loss carryforwards. Workouts out of court.
  • 8 ideas we know: NPV · DIY principle · CAPM · efficient markets · value additivity · capital structure · option theory · agency theory.
  • 10 we don't: project risk & firm goal · risk & return · efficiency exceptions · sticky dividends/flexible buybacks · why EPS attention · payout controversy/active funds · which risks to take · value of liquidity · merger waves · crisis-prone systems (house −35%, stocks −55%).

Connections & exam strategy
  • MM frictions (tax, distress, agency) explain capital structure, payout, hedging, leasing, LBO leverage.
  • Agency recurs: free cash flow/empire building (15, 17, 19, 32), risk shifting (17, 24, 33), options inducing risk (19, 21).
  • Option thread: equity = call, debt = riskless − put, convertible = bond + call, real options; higher σ raises option value and owners' risk appetite.
  • Claim & queue: seniority, covenants, SPVs, PE layers, Ch 7/11.
  • Issue signals overvaluation (14) → pecking order (17). DuPont's equity multiplier is MM II. EFN and sustainable growth depend on payout.
Valuation toolbox: WACC for steady projects · APV when financing changes · real options for flexibility · merger NPV = synergies − premium.
